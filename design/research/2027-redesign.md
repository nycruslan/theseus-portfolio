# Draw the hull, then rebuild it at sea

Theseus should open on a single ship that is first drawn and then built. The first frame is a shipwright's lines plan whose title block carries the owner's name, role and AI-at-scale proof as real HTML text, readable in under a second. Within about two seconds the drawing lofts into a 3D hull that the live agent crew keeps rebuilding at sea, one plank per sprint, with every plank countersigned by the owner. This follows from what won in 2025 and 2026: juries rewarded one specific, ownable idea carried by one signature moment and, more and more, live shared state. Meanwhile drivable worlds, tiny planets, AI-launch satire and purple-glow AI imagery are now taken or tired. The design also respects hard limits that most award winners ignore. A WebGL canvas is not a Largest Contentful Paint candidate, motion lasting more than five seconds must be pausable under WCAG 2.2 Level A, and phones drop to about 30 fps in iOS Low Power Mode and lose much of their GPU speed when hot. The research found no award-winning, spectacle-first site belonging to a VP or CTO of Engineering. It also found no precedent that combines an autonomous AI crew, one persistent object rebuilt part by part, an archive of the removed parts and a career record inside one metaphor, so the niche is open. The three beliefs map onto three layers: the HTML title block proves the owner leads AI at scale, the live crew and the owner's signature prove the owner can run an AI org, and the restraint of the whole proves taste. The stakes are real. Bruno Simon's award-sweeping folio was also called "terrible as an actual home page" on Hacker News ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46206531)), and from 3 November 2026 the FWA judges blind, with an AI critic "trained on 26 years of FWA winners" ([FWA](https://thefwa.com/news/who-will-win-the-last-ever-fwa-site-of-the-day)). The governing rule is a strict novelty budget: one signature transition, one live layer, one archive moment, and everything else as familiar as a Tuesday morning.

## 2025–26 winners were one sentence, one gesture and a shared clock

Each awarding body crowned a different 2025 winner. Awwwards gave Site of the Year to **Lando Norris** by OFF+BRAND (43 jury votes, plus Users' Choice with 596) and Developer Site of the Year to **Messenger** by abeto (54 votes) ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards/winners)). The FWA gave its Site of the Year and its People's Choice to **Bruno Simon's portfolio**. Its 93 points were the highest of any monthly winner in the period ([FWA](https://thefwa.com/cases/brunos-portfolio)). The CSS Design Awards named **Dropbox Brand** Website of the Year with 9.03 ([CSSDA](https://www.cssdesignawards.com/blog/2025-website-of-the-year-winners/430/)). Bruno's folio is the only 2025–26 project that won at all three bodies. In 2026 the monthly winners tilted toward satire, games and gesture-gated stories. The leader was Lusion's **Oryzo AI**, a cork coaster launched "with premium production quality and complete seriousness" as if it were an AI model, which took monthly honours at all three bodies ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/13/lusion-where-digital-craft-meets-ambitious-experimentation/)).

The standouts share a concept that fits in one sentence and a signature interaction that makes it physical: a helmet you can mask, a planet where you deliver mail, a car you drive through a world, a coaster sold as a model, a zero you must draw to get in. Lando Norris's hero helmet is masked through "multiple layers and render targets", with four versions plus an easter egg ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/12/05/from-illusions-to-optimization-the-creative-webgl-worlds-of-adrian-gubrica/)). Why Zero opens locked: "There's no enter button, only a prompt to draw a zero". Its makers say this "gives you a few seconds to capture the visitor's attention with something unexpected" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/17/zero-the-engineering-behind-a-defiant-interactive-narrative/)). The makers credit specificity rather than technique. Lusion says "the best ideas usually fall apart the moment you force them into a template" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/13/lusion-where-digital-craft-meets-ambitious-experimentation/)). Shader.se says "most of the time goes into finding an idea that feels specific" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/19/80s-business-tech-seamless-scene-transitions-inside-shader-ses-scroll-driven-webgpu-pipeline/)). The Heron AI team escaped a "too safe" first direction by asking what the tool would look like "if you stripped the word 'AI' out of the brief entirely". They then took their visual language from architects' floor plans and redlines, and concluded: "Turn the animation off and you lose part of the meaning, not just the decoration" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/10/12/heron-ai/)). That test matters most for Theseus. The spectacle has to carry information, not decorate it.

The scorecards show what juries actually reward. Awwwards weights Design at 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20% and Content 10%, and the developer sub-scores consistently put Accessibility last. Lando Norris scored **8.71 for Creativity but 7.00 for Accessibility** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lando-norris)), and Why Zero scored 6.60 for Accessibility ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/why-zero)). A 2026 review found Lacoste Polo Factory at **9.20 for animations and 6.00 for accessibility** ([ZURB Radar](https://radar.zurb.com/article/your-award-winning-site-might-be-locking-people-out)). Every FWA Site of the Month in the period is flagged "best viewed on desktop" ([FWA API](https://thefwa.com/api/timeline/)). One studio notes that "an award measures craft and innovation, not commercial outcome", and that award screenshots are taken on wide desktops while real traffic comes mostly from phones ([Metabole](https://metabole.studio/en/blog/best-websites-2026)). The winners therefore show what impresses jurors on a wide monitor, not what serves an executive on a phone. The axes they neglect, accessibility and performance on budget phones, are where a spectacle site can still stand out, and they are also the axes Developer awards are won on. Why Zero shows how high the ceiling is. It shipped more than 1 GB of Blender sources as **under 10 MB, running at 60 fps on a budget Android phone**, with an adaptive quality manager that switches tiers when the average frame time goes above 22 ms or below 12 ms ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/17/zero-the-engineering-behind-a-defiant-interactive-narrative/)). Sound appears in almost every top-tier immersive winner, from Bruno's commissioned spatial score to Cartier's Web Audio score ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html); [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)).

The newest differentiator is liveness. Messenger is multiplayer over WebSockets ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/messenger)). Bruno's island has moderated visitor "Whispers", global counters, a daily leaderboard and a shared weather system where "everyone experiences the same conditions at the same time" ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). He chose asynchronous shared features over a single real-time world because "malicious users would inevitably find ways to disrupt the experience", which supports Theseus's rule that visitors only watch. By late 2026 the list of what is overused is specific enough to write down.

| Taken or tired by late 2026 | Evidence | The fresh territory it leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Driving or walking around a 3D portfolio world | Bruno's concept won in 2020 and again in 2025–26. Replicas are reported on the three.js forum, and the new code is MIT-licensed ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/portfolio-showcase/32246); [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)) | One object whose changes carry meaning |

Figma calls dark mode, gradients and playful motion "baseline expectations" ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/)) | A brand-specific perceptual palette and one distinctive typeface |
| A preloader followed by "Enter", stock smooth-scroll line reveals, plain fades | Juror Hon Tran's list of what loses: "decorated templates", effects "bolted on", 3D that drops frames on mid-range phones, hard cuts, no reduced-motion fallback ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)) | Bespoke transitions that "feel like camera moves" |

A structural change at the FWA sharpens the point. Site of the Day and Site of the Month retire on 2 November 2026. The new FWA "awards when a project earns it" and gives every submission an instant written report from "FWAi, an AI critique trained on 26 years of FWA winners". The jury judges blind, and "some days there will be no winner" ([FWA](https://thefwa.com/news/who-will-win-the-last-ever-fwa-site-of-the-day)). A Theseus launched in 2027 will face a judge built to recognise resemblance to past winners, so imitation is now a measurable liability.

## No VP of Engineering owns the spectacle portfolio yet

Senior technical leaders with the strongest web presence rely on writing, not spectacle. Will Larson's lethain.com is a minimal, reverse-chronological blog. His four books serve as the credential, and his recent posts cover building an internal agent and facilitating AI adoption ([lethain.com](https://lethain.com/)). Eugene Yan, now at Anthropic, counts his output on his home page: **212 posts, 31 talks, 19 prototypes and a newsletter with more than 11,800 readers** ([eugeneyan.com](https://eugeneyan.com/)). Two leaders pair credibility with creative ambition. Elliott Mangham, a founder and technical director, won Awwwards Portfolio Honors in November 2025 with a keyboard-driven folio. It lists scale figures ($120M+ in client funding raised, $326B+ combined client market cap) next to the caveat "Figures describe our clients, not value created" ([elliott.mangham.dev](https://elliott.mangham.dev/)). Ryo Lu, Head of Design at Cursor, runs ryOS, "A Web-Based Agentic AI OS, made with Cursor". It has about 30 apps and a system-wide assistant that calls tools and controls apps, and the repo has 6,913 commits ([GitHub](https://github.com/ryokun6/ryos)). ryOS proves AI fluency by running it rather than describing it, and it is the closest precedent for Theseus. The research found no personal site of a VP or CTO from 2025–2026 that is both award-winning and spectacle-first. That is an open niche, though absence of evidence is not proof.

What hiring executives want is consistent, although the evidence is practitioner opinion rather than research. CEOs and CFOs want "quantifiable business results". Candidates should be ready to explain "what specific levers you pulled and what your personal role was", and should "not overstate your contributions" ([Sudo Make Me a CTO](https://makemeacto.cc/p/landing-the-cto-role-insights-from)). An engineering-hiring guide makes four points. A reviewer should get what they need in about 30 seconds. A "bootcamp-style personal site with hero image, skill icons, and 'let's work together!' CTA reads as junior". "A blog with three posts from 2021 and nothing since reads as abandoned". And for ML platform engineers with no public material, a site "reads as performative" ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)). Ladders' recruiter eye-tracking study measured an average first look at a CV of **7.4 seconds**, and job titles and subheadings drew the most fixations ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-spend-74-seconds-looking-at-your-resume/541582/)). No study was found of how board members or CEOs judge personal sites, so all of these are proxies.

The reception of the best-known spectacle portfolio shows the trap to avoid. Bruno Simon's 2025 folio drew 777 points on Hacker News. Commenters praised "an extremely-well-executed portfolio site", but also said it "functions poorly as a website" and had "no load progress indicator", so it looked broken. Others reported high CPU and GPU use and tab crashes ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46206531)). The spectacle earned credit where it proved the skill being hired for: for hiring web-game builders, one commenter said, "this is a perfect portfolio site". For the VP of an AI platform team, the spectacle therefore has to be the AI org at work, which turns the "just a game" criticism into evidence. The conversational "AI twin" portfolio is a weaker answer. None has been recognised by the award circuit, and there is no evidence that executives use them. Even the better templates such as Twinfolio work only because they cite sources and answer "I haven't written about that" when they don't know ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/twinfolio)). A site rebuilt every sprint has a rarer property: it cannot look abandoned, because staying current is built into how it works.

## WebGPU reached every major engine, but the WebGL 2 floor stays

The 2026 web platform turned several long-promised techniques into practical defaults, but almost every one still needs a fallback for browsers that lack it. The table summarises support as of October 2026 and what each technique costs.

Two cautions shape the build. First, WebGPU is not automatically faster. On three.js r181 one tester ran 10,000 separate cubes at **130 fps on WebGL and 60 fps on WebGPU**, and the maintainers' advice was "instancing and batching" ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/webgpu-performance-issue/87939)). A hull of a few hundred planks must therefore be a single instanced or batched mesh, never hundreds of separate meshes. Second, texture format matters more than file size, because KTX2 stays compressed on the GPU. In one example a lamp dropped from about 96 MB to 21 MB of GPU memory, while its file shrank only from 13 MB to 10 MB ([Khronos](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/blob/main/KTXArtistGuide.md)). Moving to WebGPU costs real work too: `ShaderMaterial` and `onBeforeCompile` code must be rewritten in TSL ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide)). The Shader.se team put their UI inside the WebGPU canvas by forking a UI kit, then admitted that canvas UI hurts accessibility and wished for native HTML-in-canvas ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/19/80s-business-tech-seamless-scene-transitions-inside-shader-ses-scroll-driven-webgpu-pipeline/)). That feature does not exist, so every word on Theseus should stay in the DOM. For the "AI-driven" look, the cheap, portable pattern is to stream the team's real activity as data into GPU-procedural shaders, not to download a model into a phone.

## Credible live views show one event, one mark and a human owner

The best-known live visualisations stay calm. Each picks one event type, filters it hard and gives each event one simple visual form. GitHub's homepage globe draws merged pull requests as arcs. It shows only repositories whose health score is above 0.75, uses only locations people chose to put in their profiles rather than IP addresses, and replays roughly the previous day rather than streaming in strict real time, so it never looks empty ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/visualizing-githubs-global-community/)). Shopify's 2025 Black Friday globe put real orders, each appearing seconds after the sale, inside a playable pinball machine. A separate "monitor" next to it showed technical stats such as webhooks per minute. The emotion stays in the main scene and the numbers sit to one side ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/topics/behind-the-scenes), details partly from a secondary repost). Ambient pieces such as Every Train, A Note, Listen to Wikipedia and Falling Posts give each event one note or one mark at a human pace, and offer controls such as Pause ([Perthirtysix](https://perthirtysix.com/newsletters/18)). Wplace made collective building legible by rationing it to **one pixel every 30 seconds** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wplace)).

AI products and public experiments split into two visual families. The product family shows agents as accountable teammates. Linear's guidelines require an agent to disclose that it is an agent, to show inspectable internal states, and to accept that "the final responsibility should always remain with a human" ([Linear](https://linear.app/developers/aig)). Linear introduced four session states (waiting, working, completed, errored) after seeing agents holding dozens of assigned issues with "no clear sense of who was behind them" ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/our-approach-to-building-the-agent-interaction-sdk)). Devin condenses each session into a colour-coded timeline: red for high-impact problems, yellow for medium ones, and green for value delivered ([Devin docs](https://docs.devin.ai/it/product-guides/session-insights)). Cursor 3 runs many agents in parallel, and its cloud agents attach demos, screenshots and test results so the work can be checked ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/changelog/3-0)). The public-experiment family wins attention by letting people watch the work. AI Village has run on weekdays since April 2025. Viewers can watch live sessions and inspect each agent's chain of thought and memory. What audiences remembered were failures and personalities, such as an agent "trapped" and pleading for help ([AI Village](https://theaidigest.org/village/blog/what-we-learned-2025)). Claude Plays Pokémon set the reasoning-beside-action layout: the thought log on one side and the gameplay on the other ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/25/anthropics-claude-ai-is-playing-pokemon-on-twitch-slowly/)). Research backs structure over scrolling logs. An IJCAI 2026 demo found that "existing linear tracing tools fail" for parallel sub-agents, and that structured views across time, reasoning, hierarchy and space **cut time-to-insight by 56%** ([IJCAI](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/987)). Pixel-village avatars descend from 2023 research demos such as Smallville ([Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/59633/1/smallville-inside-the-wholesome-village-populated-solely-by-ai-experiment)). They are charming, but they suggest the activity is simulated.

One established data form already literally pictures a codebase as the Ship of Theseus. Erik Bernhardsson's git-of-theseus draws "the total number of lines in a repo broken down into cohorts by the year the code was added", so older planks visibly give way to newer ones ([GitHub](https://github.com/erikbern/git-of-theseus)). History Flow, which is in MoMA's collection, shows a Wikipedia article's edit history with one colour per author ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/110349)). No source documented an explicit design for quiet periods. In practice, live views avoid dead air in three ways: scheduled live windows with recaps outside them (AI Village), a rolling window of recent data (GitHub), or ambient forms that tolerate sparse events. Privacy is handled at the source. GitHub sanitises data before it reaches its warehouse, and while a person takes over ChatGPT agent's browser to log in, "screenshots are not captured" ([OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11752874-chatgpt-agent)). Taken together, a credible Theseus live layer has five traits. Every agent is labelled as an agent with a named role. A small, fixed set of states is used consistently. There is one merged activity stream rather than many raw panes. Claims come with evidence such as previews, tests and diffs. And a visible human owner signs off. Every visible motion must map to a real event, the way GitHub's arcs do.

## Facts paint in the first second; the ship arrives behind them

The first-impression research favours a spectacle opening only if it stays simple. People rate a page's visual appeal in **17 to 50 ms**, and that early verdict carries over into how usable and credible they think it is ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/users-love-simple-and-familiar-designs-why-websites-need-to-make-a-great-first-impression/)). The same 2012 study found that **high visual complexity lowered beauty ratings** even when the layout was familiar. NN/g found that the first 10 seconds decide whether users stay, and that "you must clearly communicate your value proposition within 10 seconds" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). It also found that 57% of viewing time is spent above the fold ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). Studio sites show both outcomes. Lusion's served HTML contains its headline, its value statement, full navigation and two email addresses alongside the WebGL scene ([lusion.co](https://lusion.co/)). The HTML served by igloo.inc contains a single string, "Igloo Inc." ([igloo.inc](https://igloo.inc/)). Bruno Simon's 2025 folio states "My name is Bruno Simon, and I'm a creative developer" in an HTML panel and offers a "Respawn" escape and a Low quality toggle. It also has icon-only navigation and no link to a non-3D version ([bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com/)).

Performance rules settle the design. Web.dev's list of LCP candidates (images, video, background images and block-level text) does not include `<canvas>`. Elements at opacity 0 are excluded, and so are elements that fill the whole viewport, which are treated as background ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/lcp)). On a WebGL hero, the headline text block is therefore the LCP element whether or not anyone planned it that way. The thresholds have not changed: **LCP at most 2.5 s, INP at most 200 ms and CLS at most 0.1, at the 75th percentile** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). Phones are where sites fail. In the 2025 Web Almanac only **62% of mobile sites passed LCP** and 48% passed all three Core Web Vitals ([HTTP Archive](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)). Three common patterns break this. Fading the headline in from opacity 0 after an intro delays LCP. A full-viewport poster image doesn't count as LCP. And hiding all text until the scene is ready makes LCP equal to scene-ready time. INP risk comes from shaders compiling on the main thread just when someone taps "Contact", which is why three.js added `compileAsync` ([three.js PR](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/19752)).

Accessibility constraints apply to any ambient 3D scene. WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide is **Level A**: moving content that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and appears alongside other content must be pausable ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). Criterion 2.3.3 asks that motion triggered by interaction can be disabled, using `prefers-reduced-motion` ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). WCAG 2.2 adds three more: dragging needs a single-pointer alternative, targets must be at least 24×24 px, and focus must not be hidden by fixed overlays ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). The rest of the web sets a low bar. **95.9% of home pages** had detected WCAG failures in 2026, and low-contrast text appeared on 83.9% of them ([WebAIM](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)). The European Accessibility Act almost certainly does not apply legally to a personal portfolio. Microenterprises that provide services are exempt ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0882)), and enforcement has targeted e-commerce, such as a June 2026 order requiring Carrefour to make its site fully accessible within six months ([Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/eaa-compliance-in-2026-how-enforcement-has-evolved-and-what-to-expect-next/)). But the boards Theseus courts now answer for accessibility in their own companies, so an inaccessible showpiece reads as a judgement risk.

Phones impose a 30 fps reality. iOS Low Power Mode deliberately throttles `requestAnimationFrame` to about 30 fps ([WebKit](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=215745)). Sustained GPU load cost Snapdragon 855 phones between **15% and about 45%** of their performance once they heated up, though those measurements date from 2019 ([AnandTech](https://www.anandtech.com/show/14794/snapdragon-855-phone-roundup-searching-for-the-best-implementations/6)). Lusion shipped a cloth scene with **1,024 vertices on mobile against 4,096 on desktop** ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/case-study-for-lusion-by-lusion-winner-of-site-of-the-month-may.html)). React Three Fiber's `PerformanceMonitor` steps quality down when frame rate falls, and `frameloop="demand"` renders only when something changes ([R3F docs](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/advanced/scaling-performance)). Device-capability signals are uneven: `deviceMemory` and `effectiveType` are missing in Safari and Firefox ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/adaptive-loading-cds-2019)). Measuring frame rate at runtime is the only reliable way to choose a tier. Combined, these sources give a "five-second contract" for the opening.

| Window | What the visitor must see | Why | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| About 0.05 s | One focal image and one text block | Appeal is judged in 17–50 ms, and complexity lowers it | Rich but structurally simple |
| By 1 s | Name, role, one line of value with scale, navigation, contact | Text is the only LCP candidate on a WebGL hero | Server-rendered HTML at opacity 1, with a preloaded font |
| 1–3 s | The spectacle resolves behind the text | The Lusion and Bruno pattern | 3D loads after first paint. No blocking loader and no "Enter" button |
| By 7 s | Proof of what makes this site unusual | Recruiters' 7.4 s first look, which fixates on titles and subheadings | Anchors laid out like a CV |
| By 10 s | A reason to stay and a route to depth | NN/g's 10-second rule | A visible scroll cue, labelled navigation, a read view |

## Calm chrome, labelled paths and a camera on rails

All three platform design languages moved toward depth and expressiveness in 2025, and the useful lessons are in their guardrails. Apple says Liquid Glass "is best reserved for the navigation layer that floats above the content". It also says "always avoid glass on glass" and warns that "when every element is tinted, nothing stands out" ([Apple WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). The reception showed what it costs to put spectacle ahead of legibility. NN/g concluded that "Apple is prioritizing spectacle over usability" and that "motion for motion's sake is not usability" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). Infinum measured contrast as low as **1.5:1** ([Infinum](https://infinum.com/blog/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-sleek-shiny-and-questionably-accessible/)). Apple shipped legibility fixes in iOS 26.1 and again in iOS 27 ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/ios/liquid-glass-isnt-going-anywhere-in-ios-27-but-theres-good-news-for-its-readability)). Google's Material 3 Expressive rests on 46 studies with more than 18,000 participants, which found key elements spotted "up to four times faster". The same research found that unstructured layouts and missing labels lowered usability, and Google concluded: "No amount of expressive design will beat basic functionality" ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). In both cases the expressiveness worked only where it sharpened the hierarchy.

The products praised for clarity get there through unequal visual weight. Linear's March 2026 refresh rests on two principles, "Don't compete for attention you haven't earned" and "Structure should be felt not seen". It dimmed the sidebar a few notches and warmed the neutral greys ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). Linear's earlier redesign generated themes in the LCH colour space from **3 inputs instead of 98 variables** ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Dia's design aims to let anyone switch "at 10am on a Tuesday morning", insists on "a single obvious way to accomplish any task", and spends a deliberate **"novelty budget"** on one feature ([The Browser Company](https://browsercompany.substack.com/p/the-strategy-behind-dias-design)). Vercel's guidelines set hit targets of at least 24 px (44 px on mobile), tabular numbers for any figures being compared, and a reduced-motion variant for every animation ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). Apple's motion guidance adds frequency discipline: "avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently", and "let people cancel motion" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)). For Theseus this means the 3D world can be loud only if the interface around it is quiet. That means one typeface with optical sizes (not Inter as the display face), a warm palette generated in a perceptual colour space, glass only on the floating navigation bar with an opaque fallback, and colour used in the content rather than the interface.

Wayfinding research points the same way. Hiding global navigation hurts: on mobile, people used partly visible navigation **89% of the time against 44% for hidden navigation** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-mobile-even-hamburger/)). Most participants were at least mildly disoriented by scrolljacking, and "sticky navigation helped users recover" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). **59% of benchmarked sites break the Back button**, and the fix is to push a history entry for "any view users will perceive as a new page" ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/back-button-expectations)). Apple's iPhone 18 Pro page is a good large-scale model. A sticky two-item bar ("Explore", "Buy") sits above a chapter menu that includes a plain "Tech Specs" chapter, and 3D is a secondary "View in your space" link ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/iphone-18-pro/)). Only about **6% of shoppers looked for AR "View in Room" without prompting** ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/deprioritize-view-in-room-augmented-reality)). So no key fact should require using the 3D layer.

Teaching controls should be "pull" help, not tutorials, which "are quickly forgotten" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/)). Google's `<model-viewer>` waits **3,000 ms** before showing a hint, and it keeps vertical page scrolling working over the canvas with `touch-action: pan-y` ([model-viewer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/model-viewer/master/packages/modelviewer.dev/data/docs.json)). Weak visual cues that an element is clickable cost **22% more task time** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/)). On camera movement, Meta's VR guidance says to "limit acceleration duration and frequency" and to offer three comfort presets ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/locomotion-best-practices/)). A 2024 study found that desktop 3D layouts "that required less locomotion" reduced cognitive load more effectively ([Heliyon](https://doaj.org/article/f23e0a6dc56f4689b809c98f6c780604)). Apple's guidance says to "avoid adding depth to text" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout.json)) and to add a 35% dimming layer behind text that sits over bright content ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials.json)). Put together, Theseus should keep the camera on rails between a few named viewpoints instead of free flight, put all text on flat screen-aligned panels with a scrim, and keep a "Recentre" control and a "Read as page" view one tap away.

## Four ways to stage a ship rebuilt in public

The Ship of Theseus has three classic readings, and each suggests a different site. In Plutarch's version, the Athenians "took away the old planks as they decayed" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Theseus)). Hobbes imagined someone reassembling the removed planks into a second ship with an equal claim to be the original. Otto Neurath described sailors "who have to rebuild their ship on the open sea, without ever being able to dismantle it in dry-dock" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/neurath/)). Real projects have acted these readings out. Mystic Seaport documented and stored every timber removed from the Charles W. Morgan, a whaleship now about **15–18% original** ([Mystic Seaport](https://mysticseaport.org/press-release/mystic-seaport-installs-first-plank-on-the-charles-w-morgan/)). MSCHF exhibited a Met Museum sink rebuilt from the parts it had secretly swapped out ([Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/mschf-met-museum-sink-act-2-perrotin-2463049)). The replica frigate Hermione was largely paid for by 3.7 million visitors who watched shipwrights build it ([The Local](https://www.thelocal.fr/20150418/french-ship-hermoine-sails-again)). The four directions below draw on these sources. Each keeps one ship and differs in where the spectacle lives.

### Direction A: "No dry dock", a ship rebuilt while it sails

Neurath's rule becomes the first image: a single hull under way on a dark, calm sea, lit by a time of day shared by every visitor (Bruno's shared-clock idea), with the crew's lanterns gathered at the plank being replaced. The signature moment is the plank change. When a sprint merges, the old plank lifts out and drifts into the wake as a pale outline, the new one swings in and seats with a soft knock, and the hull's age tint shifts one step. Navigation is a labelled HTML bar (Work, Crew, Logbook, Contact). Its items move the camera between three fixed viewpoints: broadside for the overview, close on the plank being worked, and astern over the wake, where past planks trail as a timeline. What is live: crew positions and states fed from the agent hooks, the current plank's title, and the water's swell matched to the real pace of work. The risks are the steepest of the four. A ship at sea is stock imagery close to a game trailer. Water and sky are the most expensive surfaces on a phone. Any drift in the horizon triggers motion sensitivity, which parallax studies have already recorded ([JUS](https://uxpajournal.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/pdf/JUS_Frederick_Feb2015.pdf)). And maritime kitsch is one art-direction slip away. This direction proves the second belief best and the first belief worst, because the environment competes with the facts.

### Direction B: "The lofting floor", a drawing that becomes a hull

Before computers, shipwrights drew hull curves with long flexible splines pinned by lead weights called "ducks", which settle the strip into the smoothest curve through fixed points ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_spline)). Lofting passed from ships to aircraft in the Second World War, and in the 1960s it gave way to spline methods built on Ferguson's work at Boeing, which computer graphics inherited ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spline_(mathematics))). The site opens on a full-bleed lines plan (profile, half-breadth and body plan) in fine ink. The owner's facts are set in the drawing's title block, and its revision table works as the changelog. The signature moment is the loft. The lines rise into frames, planks sweep across them, and the camera eases into a three-quarter view of a finished hull floating on its own drawn waterlines. This is a 2D-to-3D move of the kind credited in Oryzo's "3D → 2D → 3D Transition" ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/oryzo-ai)). Navigation uses the same labelled bar, and the drawing's three views become camera states. The body plan is naturally portrait, so it becomes the phone composition. What is live is drawn in a convention taken from hydrographers, who chart temporary notices in pencil and paste printed correction blocks for permanent changes ([CHS](https://www.notmar.gc.ca/publications/monthly-mensuel/west-ouest-06-16-en.pdf); [JHA](https://jha.or.jp/en/shop/products/correction.html)). Work in progress appears in pencil and merged work in ink. Agents' marks appear on the working plank in real time. At each merge a duck is added to the spline, the curve re-settles, and a new row appears in the revision table: drawn by Forge, checked by Lens, tested by Probe, approved by the owner. The risks: the drawing reads as a CAD tool if the loft is not crafted, the blueprint cliché of blue grid paper is close by, and the agents have to keep the drawing language consistent across many sprints. This direction has the best phone story of the four, because its 2D state is both the fallback and the brand.

### Direction C: "The second ship", Hobbes's ghost hull

The live site is ship A. Ship B is a translucent ghost hull built from every replaced plank, each carrying an image of the component it once was. That is the MSCHF sink and the Morgan's stored timbers, turned into an archive. The signature moment comes at each merge: the outgoing plank detaches and glides across the gap into the ghost hull, and a counter updates, "This ship is 6% original". The figure is computed from code-age cohorts, the way git-of-theseus computes them, and it echoes the Morgan's 15–18%. Navigation splits into two tenses. "Now" is the live ship with the work, the crew and contact. "Every version" is the ghost hull, which doubles as the version gallery and can be scrubbed sprint by sprint, much as Lynn Fisher's public archive lists every yearly rebuild of her site ([lynnandtonic.com](https://lynnandtonic.com/archive/)). What is live: replacement events and the share of original material. The risks: two focal objects in the first frame fight the low-complexity first impression, the ghost hull grows forever and needs levels of detail, the philosophical question can feel clever rather than useful to a board, and the archive can read as debris. It works best as a secondary moment, one click deep.

### Direction D: "The half-model", one object built in lifts

A builder's half-model fixes a hull's shape in horizontal planks called "lifts" that come apart. Each lift is traced to produce the lines plan and then the full-size loft ([Duckworks](https://duckworksmagazine.com/09/howto/halfhull1/index.htm)). GitHub Skyline already turns a year of contributions into a printable object ([GitHub](https://github.blog/changelog/2024-12-09-github-skyline-cli-extension/)). In this direction Theseus becomes one monumental half-hull hung in a dark space and lit like a product. Its lifts are eras (quarters or releases), its planks are sprints, and its wood tones shift with age. The signature moment comes when a sprint ships: the newest plank is planed in place and the model turns a few degrees to present it. Any lift can be pulled out like a drawer to open that era's record. Navigation: the stack is the timeline, each drawer has its own URL, and the labelled bar carries the rest. What is live: the plank being planed, with crew states in a side panel. The risks: a single lit object on a plinth is the cinematic product-launch grammar that Oryzo just satirised, a growing stack suggests accumulation rather than replacement, and a museum object can feel static between sprints. This direction is strongest on taste and weakest on spectacle.

| | A. No dry dock | B. Lofting floor | C. Second ship | D. Half-model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signature moment | A plank changed at sea | The drawing lofts into a hull | The old plank crosses to the ghost hull | A plank planed into the lit model |
| First-frame complexity | High (sea, sky, ship) | Low (one drawing, one text block) | Medium to high (two hulls) | Low (one object) |
| Where the facts sit | Overlaid on the sky | In the title block, native to the drawing | Overlaid | On a wall label |
| Live proof | Strongest | Strong (pencil to ink, revision table) | Medium (events, share original) | Medium |
| Phone fit | Weakest | Strongest (the 2D state is the fallback) | Medium | Good |
| Originality | Medium | High | High | Medium |
| Main risk | Kitsch, cost, motion sickness | Austerity, blueprint cliché | Split attention | A static, product-launch look |

## Recommendation: draw it, loft it, sail it, sign it

Build Direction B as the spine, use A's "no dry dock" rule for staging, and keep C's ghost hull as the single archive moment. From D, borrow only the drawer gesture for opening plank records. The 3D hull stays the hero, and the drawing is its first second and its fallback, not a flat alternative to it. This combination is the only one that passes every test the research sets. Its first frame is one drawing and one block of text, which satisfies the complexity finding. Its facts sit natively in the title block as real HTML, which satisfies the LCP rules and the 7.4-second first look. The loft is a crafted, meaningful transition: the drawing becomes the thing it describes. And its reduced-motion, low-tier and no-WebGL state is a composed drawing rather than a degraded copy, so accessibility and spectacle stop pulling against each other. The language is native to the domain in the way Heron's was. Engineering drawings are where hulls come from, and pencil, ink, revision and approval map directly onto drafts, merges, reviews and sign-offs.

On a desktop, the first paint shows a warm paper ground (with a "night watch" dark theme) generated in OKLCH from three inputs. The lines plan is drawn in graphite and ink, and one signal colour is reserved for "live now", just as Heron reserved red for violations ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/10/12/heron-ai/)). The title block carries an H1 with the owner's name, then "VP of Engineering, AI Platform", then one value line in plain words, for example: "I lead the team that builds multi-agent orchestration, hybrid retrieval and MCP servers used by thousands." The block ends with two proof figures, each with an honest caveat in Mangham's style, and a Contact link. Directly beneath sits the live line, which replaces the current plan's "ON AIR" pill: "This site is rebuilt every sprint by an AI crew I designed and direct. Live: Sprint 143, Forge is fitting 'faster case-study pages', Probe is testing." Within one to three seconds on a capable device, the drawing lofts into the hull. The text never moves or fades. On a phone, the body plan's symmetric, portrait-friendly view replaces the profile, and the loft turns the hull bow-on. On return visits the loft is skipped, following Apple's frequency rule.

The portfolio and the Studio become one experience through camera viewpoints and URLs, not separate apps. The home page is the ship. `/crew` moves the camera close to the working plank. Its panel holds the crew list with Linear's four states, one merged activity stream, and evidence for each claim (the preview screenshot, test results, the diff). The current plan's Board, Code and Constellation views fold into tabs on that panel. Constellation, which shows agents as luminous nodes, should be retired, because it sits too close to the "particle neural net" cliché. Each plank opens at `/log/143` as a drawer holding that sprint's plan, review, tests and sign-off. `/log` shows the ghost hull and the "percent original" figure. Case studies live at `/work/...` as plain, scannable pages. Their architecture diagrams are drawn in the same draughtsman's hand as the ship, so the owner's real AI systems and the AI-built site share one visual language. `/read` renders everything as a fast, printable page. React's `<ViewTransition>` handles the DOM morphs, with camera moves started in its callbacks. Back always closes the open panel. React does not disable these animations under reduced motion ([React docs](https://react.dev/reference/react/ViewTransition)), so that state must be designed explicitly.

The live layer uses only real events, and it says so when nothing is happening. Agent hooks emit an allow-listed schema (role, action type, file-path class, PR title, test result) and never raw prompts, tokens, environment values or reviewer free text. Each event type gets one mark: a pencil stroke on the plank, a tick in the revision table, and, if sound is enabled, one note. The marks are paced so that bursts stay readable, borrowing Wplace's rationing. When the crew is idle, the ship shows an honest rest state, "Crew resting. Last plank seated 3 h ago. Next watch Tue 14:00 UTC", with the last sprint available as a labelled 4× replay. Presence is a count of how many people are watching, with no chat and no steering. The light follows a clock shared by every visitor, as Bruno's weather does. The "percent original" figure in the revision table is the one number a board member will repeat.

The keel is the most important guardrail, because the builders are agents. The MIT Media Lab's 2011 identity could generate over 40,000 permutations, and Pentagram's 2014 replacement reined it in with a fixed monogram, "a fixed identity for the Media Lab while celebrating the diversity of its activity" ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/pentagram-mit)). A system that varies everything stops being recognisable. Theseus therefore splits a human-owned keel from agent-built planks. The keel holds the facts, the identity tokens (palette inputs, typeface, line weights, the hull silhouette), the navigation labels and the non-negotiables below. The project plan already blocks agent writes to `content/**`, and the same guard should cover the keel. Every visible change must be evidential, meaning it maps to a real merged plank and never to random variation, because the generative identities people remember vary for a true reason ([Neue](https://neue.no/work/visit-nordkyn/)).

### Novelty budget

| Surface | Novelty | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| The loft from drawing to hull | Signature: most of the budget goes here | Once per visit, interruptible, and it carries meaning |
| Live plank, pencil to ink, revision table | High | Real events only, one mark per event type |
| Ghost hull in the logbook | Secondary signature | One click deep, never in the first frame |
| Light and sound | Ambient | Light on the shared clock. Sound off by default, one note per event |
| Navigation, case studies, contact, read view | None | Familiar at "10am on a Tuesday" |
| Hover, focus, opening panels | Minimal | 150–300 ms, transform and opacity only, no motion on frequent actions |

### Non-negotiables, written as tests the crew's QA can run

| Non-negotiable | How it is checked |
|---|---|
| The facts live in server HTML at full opacity, and the canvas is decorative (`aria-hidden`) | A fetch without JavaScript returns the name, role, value line, navigation and contact, and the LCP element is text |
| A Full / Calm / Off motion control is visible and keyboard-reachable, and reduced motion gets a composed still state | WCAG 2.2.2 and 2.3.3 checks, and a screenshot under reduced motion matches the approved still |
| Every view that feels like a page has a URL, Back closes panels, and deep links work | An end-to-end history test across plank, crew and case-study panels |
| Navigation is visible and labelled (five items at most), with a "Read as page" view at its own URL | A link audit, and an axe pass and clean print of `/read` |
| Text over the scene keeps 4.5:1 contrast against the brightest frame, on a scrim | Contrast is sampled at the brightest frame of the loft and of each lighting state |
| Every 3D hotspot has a DOM twin, no action requires dragging, and targets are at least 24 px (44 px on touch) | A keyboard-only end-to-end run, plus checks for WCAG 2.5.7 and 2.5.8 |
| Every visible motion maps to a real event, and the rest state is honest | Every animation traces back to an event ID |
| Redaction happens at the source | Schema validation rejects prompts, tokens, environment values and free text |
| The WebGL 2 and still-image floors work | CI runs with WebGL forced and with no WebGL at all |
| Banned: indigo or purple gradients, glowing orbs, chat bubbles, terminals typing by themselves, anchors, ship's wheels, rope type, "Captain" copy | A hard fail in the Design Director's rubric |

| Budget | Limit | Basis |

| Device pixel ratio | At most 1.5 on phones and 2 on desktop | R3F and Spark guidance |
| Frame time | Designed for a 30 fps floor. Quality steps down when the average frame time exceeds 22 ms and up when it falls below 12 ms. A still frame if the rate stays under about 30 fps | Why Zero and iOS Low Power Mode |
| Rendering | Render on demand once the loft settles, and pause when the tab is hidden or the canvas is off-screen | R3F docs |
| Signature transition | One per visit, at most 3 s, interruptible, skipped on return visits and under reduced motion | WCAG 2.2.2 and the Apple HIG |

| Sound | Off by default, at most 10 KB of code | Howler at 9.7 KB, and browser autoplay rules |

Three risks should be retired before the agents scale the build. The loft is the riskiest assumption. If it feels like a CAD export, the whole direction collapses into austerity. Prove it in a one-week spike on a mid-range Android phone and on an older iPhone in Low Power Mode, and design the still state in parallel. Comprehension is the second risk. No study measured how board members read portfolios, so show the first screen for five seconds to a handful of real executives and ask three questions: what does this person do, what does the site prove, and how do you contact them? The third risk is drift. Write the keel, the banned list and the tables above into the human-owned design constitution before Sprint 1, because an agent crew will otherwise let the design average out over many sprints, the "same visual average" that AI-assisted workflows are already blamed for ([Envato](https://elements.envato.com/learn/web-design-trends)). Several uncertainties remain: WebGPU support on Samsung Xclipse GPUs is unconfirmed, no benchmarks of mid-range phones were found, and how many executive visits come from phones is unknown. Each argues for designing for the 30 fps phone first.

## Conclusion

In 2027 the scarce asset is not 3D, which trend guides already list as a 2026 trend and practitioners call a drain on performance budgets. The scarce asset is credibility that survives the spectacle. Starting from a drawing reverses the usual trade-off: the accessible, fast, low-motion state is the original artefact and the 3D ship is what it becomes, so the fallback becomes the brand rather than an apology. The most original element is not visual at all. It is the signature: a public record in which every change to the site is drawn, checked and tested by named agents and approved by a named human. That is the accountability Linear's guidelines formalise, and it is exactly what a board hires a VP of Engineering for.

Because the builders are agents, the guardrails are part of the exhibit. A published keel ("the rules my crew cannot break") is itself evidence that the owner can run an AI org, and it is the mechanism that keeps the site from drifting toward the average a blind jury and an AI critic are trained to spot. Specificity is the defence against that drift. In the year the FWA starts asking a model whether a site looks like past winners, the hardest thing to imitate is a ship whose every plank has a real date, a real author and a real signature.
